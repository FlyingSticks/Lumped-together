Reach and Turn — the two registers on one line

Reach counts: equal steps mean equal amounts, its move is the slide, its involution the fold, and ∞ stays at ∞. Turn compares: equal steps mean equal ratios, its move is the scale, its involution the mirror, and ∞ is a marked point. Theorem 0 says no one graduation does both; the cross-ratio is what survives the change of register; the seam is where the two balances agree.

ReachThe additive register — the reading that counts. On a line, a coordinate in which equal steps mean equal amounts; the move that preserves it is the slide (translation), which keeps every difference and breaks every ratio. Its distinguished point is the plane at infinity, which it never marks: ∞ is pinned at ∞, the coordinate is affine, and the only involution available is the Complement. Reach is the register of the count, the covector, the stack of level planes — the glide's "equal steps, ∞ unmarked."
TurnThe multiplicative register — the reading that compares by ratio. Its move is the scale, which keeps every ratio and breaks every difference; its involution is the Reciprocal, inside exchanged with outside across the mirror circle. On a line it is a Möbius coordinate: ∞ is a marked point, so some finite point (the station, the eye) must be sent to ∞ in its place, and equal steps in depth become the harmonic progression. Turn is the register of foreshortening, of perspective, of the cross-slit rig with both feet finite.
Complement τ · x ↦ 1 − xReach's involution: the fold about the midpoint of the unit. Fixes ½ and ∞. The only element of S₃ that an affine engine can see.
Reciprocal ρ · x ↦ 1/xTurn's involution: inversion in the mirror circle of radius 1. Fixes ±1, exchanges 0 and ∞. Composed with τ it gives the 3-cycle ω = 1/(1 − x); together they generate the whole anharmonic group.
Theorem 0No single scale marks both equal steps and a finite ∞. Affine maps fix ∞; a Möbius map that brings ∞ to a finite mark is not affine. Band 3 makes it quantitative: over equal steps 0, unit, 2·unit, the second difference of the graduation times the finite mark of ∞ equals 2(1 + d)/(2 + d) ≥ 1, d = D/unit. Either failure can be driven to zero — never both. A reading is therefore a law plus a gauge class; Reach and Turn are the two classes.
the seam · the neutral surfaceWhere the two registers agree. In the split rectangle: the product balance (ac)(bd) = (ad)(bc) holds for every P (Turn); the area balance ad = bc closes only when a : b = c : d, P on the diagonal (Reach; Euclid I.43). On the congruence square this is the diagonal rᵤ = rᵥ where the two readings rule one ray with reciprocal graduations (identification check-pending per the document). Off the seam the two rulers of one line are the two readings of one congruence.
the six special values · {−1, 0, ½, 1, 2, ∞}The fixed pairs of the three involutions: τ fixes ½ and ∞, ρ fixes 1 and −1, the Third (x/(x−1)) fixes 0 and 2. One S₃ orbit on the sphere; the harmonic case. The 3-cycle ω rotates the three fundamental planes of the perspective apparatus — picture plane, station, plane at infinity.
the harmonic ladderIntegers on the Reach side, reciprocals on the Turn side, one transversal carrying each n to 1/n: the von Staudt net, buildable with parallels and incidence alone. The Reciprocal swaps the two ladders; grain lives on ℚ.
free versus weldedThe glide's third pin is the drafter's, so a depth line may be counted (Reach) or foreshortened (Turn) at will — the gauge is free. The cross-slit's third pin is a slit foot, placed by optics — the gauge is welded to the law. That single difference is the whole of "glide constructions ⇄ cross-slit geometry."
