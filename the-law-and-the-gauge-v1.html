The law and the gauge — where the seam lets glide be welded

Two readings of one congruence. The cross-slit reading is optics: its armature law (which line) and its depth gauge (where the level marks sit) are welded together and cannot be pulled apart. The glide reading carries the same kind of law with the gauge left free — the marks may be slid along the line at will. What the congruence square sorts is not welded-versus-free; that is fixed by the reading wherever you stand. It sorts whether the two laws agree. On the seam they rule the same straight line, and turning glide's free gauge to the optical one welds the readings into one map. Off the seam the camera's line bows away, and no gauge can follow it.

What to try

Sit on the diagonal. The teal line and the purple curve are the same line — one law. With the gauge at Reach the marks disagree (reciprocal graduations, the "lines agree, maps don't" level). Turn the dial to Turn: the glide marks slide along their own straight line and land on the camera's marks. That is the weld, available to glide by choice — the harmonic chip of sheet 39.

Now step off the seam. The curve bows off the line (the law gap is a unit multiple of Δ = rᵤrᵥ(rᵥ−rᵤ)). Turn the dial anyway: the marks still slide along the straight line, one channel closes, the other cannot — there is no point on the teal line for them to reach. The gauge question never arises because the laws already disagree.

On a pushbroom edge (rᵥ = 0) the v-channel is immune, so welding to u closes both; welding to v leaves u open. Shrink λ*: the gap closes as the square of the slab depth, because the curve is tangent to the line at the picture plane everywhere in the square — the picture plane is the fused locus in the depth direction, as the seam is in the (rᵤ, rᵥ) direction.

Convention: along a ray at footprint F, glide G(μ) = F + μ·d with d = (x₀rᵤ, y₀rᵥ); camera C(λ) = (x₀/(1+rᵤλ), y₀/(1+rᵥλ)); product of factors 1 per channel.
