What is drawn. The crimson quadrilateral is the wood frame, corners A B C D in order. The grey lines are the nylon: the bilinear surface, straight along every ruling, curved everywhere else. The purple curve is the diagonal drawn on the fabric from A to C; the dashed line under it is the straight chord A–C through the air. The gap between their midpoints is the sagitta.

The corners are positions. From the origin O each corner is an arrow O→A, O→B, … (the thin gold arrows). A − B is then the edge from B to A. The teal chain walks +A, −B, +C, −D head to tail from O; where it ends is T. A parallelogram walks back to O and T = 0.

What the ledger checks. The fabric midpoint is (A+B+C+D)/4, the chord midpoint is (A+C)/2; their difference is −T/4, so the sagitta is |T|/4 — measured live, not assumed. T is also twice the vector between the two chord midpoints. Note that a flat trapezoid still has T ≠ 0: T vanishes for parallelograms, not merely for flat frames — the diagonal still bows, in the plane.
