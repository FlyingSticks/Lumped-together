what this shows

Every point of the square is one line congruence carrying two readings. The ray reading collapses each congruence line to its pierce-point — a camera: pinhole on the diagonal, pushbroom and orthographic on the r = 0 edges, cross-slit everywhere else. The edge reading draws each line as a graduated projector — the glide construction. Per channel the two gauges are reciprocal, (1−rλ) against 1/(1−rλ), product 1: that identity holds at every point of the square and is the product balance.

The gold locus — the diagonal plus the two r = 0 edges — is where the separation rurv|rv−ru| closes. There the two readings rule the same line family (the ray curves straighten onto the glide lines, opposite graduations on one stroke) and the construction is unambiguously optical. Off the locus — the whole interior and the r = 1 edges — the point is doubly occupied: the ray reading is a buildable cross-slit camera, the edge reading is a glide no camera produces, and the purple depth curves bow away from the red lines by exactly the amount the separation meter reports. Fused against split, not optics against drafting: the camera goes everywhere the glide goes; only on the locus are they the same picture.
